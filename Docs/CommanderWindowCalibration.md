# Commander window and LPD calibration

The commander forward window is part of the guidance interface. Its scene geometry, the two LPD marking layers, the crew eye datum, and Luminary's N64 look angle must describe one optical system.

## Controlling sources

1. **Grumman LM Training, Course 30915, 15 February 1967**
   - Figure T30915-30 defines Apollo body stations: +X overhead, +Y toward the commander side, and +Z forward.
   - Figure T30915-39 gives the commander's flight design eye at X 279.25, Y 22, Z 54 inches and plots the front-window visibility envelope from that eye.
   - Figure T30915-38 shows the flight LPD layout and the separate inner and outer panes.
   - Scan: https://www.ibiblio.org/apollo/Documents/lm_structures_study_guide.pdf
   - Retrieved scan SHA-256: `0efc74091c24db547bcbc634124a21f8e2b3fa0c0e14e59d0339a6b868768108`
2. **NASA TN D-7439, Apollo Experience Report: Spacecraft Structural Windows, September 1973**
   - The two forward panes are flat 25 by 28 by 24 inch triangles in planes oblique to the vehicle axes.
   - Their design visibility is approximately 65 degrees down and 80 degrees outboard.
   - Each window has a structural inner pane and protective outer pane separated by a cavity vented to space.
   - Source: https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19730023038
   - Retrieved PDF SHA-256: `341effa41329abc44590e17d89110699f25fb74a9c6dc1919e460e66244193d8`
3. **NASA TN D-6846, Apollo Lunar Descent and Ascent Trajectories**
   - The computer displays the landing-site look angle relative to the LM forward body axis.
   - Apollo 11 redesignation uses 0.5 degree in-plane and 2 degree cross-range increments.
   - Source: https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19700026504

## Scene reconstruction

`LMLandingPointDesignator` maps Apollo body stations into the scene convention `+X LMP/right, +Y overhead, -Z forward`. The body-station origin remains independent of scene placement, so the procedural cabin and an artist cabin can share the same spacecraft truth while the experience positions the vehicle around the user.

The fallback inner pane is reconstructed from three design-eye rays digitized from T30915-39:

| Corner | Heading | Elevation |
|---|---:|---:|
| Upper outboard | 75° outboard | 0° |
| Upper inboard | 11° inboard | 5° up |
| Lower | 5° inboard | 65° down |

Ray distances are solved so the top, inboard, and outboard sides are 28, 24, and 25 inches. This produces an oblique pane rather than the old facade-parallel approximation. The outer-pane marks are generated by intersecting the same sight rays with a parallel outer plane. At the flight design eye, both colored marking layers collimate. Moving away from the design eye produces parallax, as it did in the LM.

The static grid remains physical window artwork. Each pane has the 0 through 60 degree elevation line and one plus/minus 10 degree cross-range bar at zero, as drawn in T30915-38. The second colored copy visible from a displaced camera is the other pane, not another bar at 50 degrees. Luminary N64 supplies the angle the LMP reads to the commander; it does not move the grid. A training overlay may identify the called angle, but it must remain visually distinct from the flight markings and must not alter guidance.

## Artist asset datum contract

An `ApolloLMCabin.usdz` uses meters and identity root scale. The following optical nodes are mandatory:

- `CDR_Eye`: origin at the mapped T30915-39 design eye.
- `CDR_Window_Inner`: origin at the upper-outboard pane datum; local +Z points toward the design eye.
- `CDR_Window_Outer`: the corresponding outer-pane datum and normal.
- `LPD_Inner` and `LPD_Outer`: separate geometry or decal layers. Do not flatten them into one texture.

The importer rejects eye or pane positions outside 3 mm and pane normals outside 0.25 degrees. This makes the window geometry a versioned handoff contract rather than an artist eyeball target.

## Remaining measurement gate

The cited primary sources do not dimension the inter-pane cavity in the available figures. The procedural fallback therefore keeps its 20 mm separation isolated as `provisionalPaneSeparationMeters`. It is not flight-certified. Replace it only with a dimensioned Grumman production drawing or a calibrated survey of a flight-configured artifact, then repeat stereo/on-head collimation at the commander design eye.

The three corner angles are readings from the plotted 1967 visibility envelope rather than dimension callouts. They are adequate to replace the prior flat-window placeholder and to constrain artist work, but a production drawing should supersede them when available.
